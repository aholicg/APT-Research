Calling people the weakest link is convenient because it moves responsibility away from the system. It turns a design failure into a personality flaw: the user should have looked more carefully, remembered one more rule, or distrusted one more message.

But people respond to the incentives and signals an interface gives them. If routine work generates constant warnings, dismissing warnings becomes routine work. If every action looks equally urgent, urgency loses meaning.

## Behavior has an environment

Security training asks users to pause, inspect, and verify. Product design asks them to move quickly, reduce friction, and trust familiar surfaces. An attacker does not need to defeat the training directly; they only need to imitate the environment.

> A secure interface makes the safe action legible before it asks a person to be vigilant.

Good systems reveal consequence at the decision point. They separate identity from presentation, make exceptional actions feel exceptional, and offer a cheap path to verification.

The human layer is not noise around the technical system. It is part of the system—and it deserves the same care we give protocols, permissions, and code.
